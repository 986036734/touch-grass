## Lakeside Light & Last Calls — Aohai, Olympic Forest Park

- **When:** Today, **17:05 → 17:50** (45 min). Beijing local time. Sunset is **17:46**, so this window catches warm pre-sunset light and ends in the afterglow. Sky is **overcast (21°C, 10 km/h wind) with 0% precipitation across the next 12 hours** — dry, calm, and no rain risk at all. Honest caveat: overcast may swallow the sun disc itself. That's fine — it's actually *better* for birds and gives you soft, even light and clean silhouettes. If the cloud deck tears open low on the horizon, you get a real show instead.

- **Where:** Olympic Forest Park, South Park (南区) — the **east shore of Aohai (奥海)**, the big lake, looking west across the water toward the tree line and the hill. Enter at the **south gate (南门)**, right by Metro Line 8's Forest Park South Gate station. Head left/northwest to the water — about 8 minutes' walk.

- **The plan:**
  - **17:05** — Through the south gate, walk northwest to the Aohai shoreline. No phone yet.
  - **17:10–17:28** — Bird the water's edge and the reed/wetland strip. Stand still for 60-second stretches; birds move the moment you stop being a moving object. Binoculars or just naked eye both work.
  - **17:28–17:44** — Walk 5 minutes north along the east shore to a west-facing gap in the trees. This is your photo position. Lower your camera, compose the frame, and just watch the light change for a few minutes before shooting.
  - **17:44–17:50** — Shoot the sunset and afterglow; the best color usually lands 5–10 minutes *after* the official 17:46 sunset. Then walk back out.

- **Look for:**
  - **Grey herons and great egrets** standing dead-still in the shallows — the park's most reliable "wow" bird.
  - **Little grebes and Eurasian coots** on open water, usually just dark commas with white tail-flashes until they dive.
  - **Azure-winged magpies** — long-tailed, blue-grey, noisy; they move in family groups through the lakeside treeline and are hard to miss once you hear them.
  - **The lake going two-tone:** wind at 10 km/h means the water won't be glass — watch the riffles catch pale sky while the faster ripples behind them stay dark.
  - **Autumn arrivals:** early October is when Beijing's winter visitors trickle in and migrants pass through. Any small, restless bird working the willow branches is worth 20 seconds of your attention.

- **Leave the phone:** Airplane mode in your pocket from the gate until 17:44 — the camera is the *only* phone use, and it starts when the light does. You're here for the 8 minutes of real sunset, not the 40 minutes of preparing to photograph it.

- **Rain check:** Not needed today — 0% precipitation. If the wind picks up or the sky fully greys out, drop the sunset shot and walk the nearby wetland boardwalk under the canopy instead; birding in overcast is unaffected.
